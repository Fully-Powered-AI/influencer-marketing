# Terms checklist: what a consumer-brand creator agreement covers, and why

Dated 2026-10-20. Version 0.1.1, for terms-sheet template 0.1.1 and programme template 0.1.2. Not legal advice.

The IDs below are the section numbers of `../assets/terms-sheet-template.md`, and `../scripts/check_terms.py` requires every one. Facts about law, platforms and Shopify come from the pack's sourced references, read on 2026-10-20: "notes" is the pack's `references/platform-and-compliance-notes.md` and "rails" is its `references/shopify-affiliate-rails.md`. Section 3 below carries the few facts a terms sheet needs, but this public edition must be installed with its sibling skills and shared references. Re-check a fact at its source before relying on it.

Origin of each rule: `[programme s.N]` the owner's programme file; `[notes s.N]` or `[rails s.N]` the pack's sourced references; `[pack]` the pack's contract or schemas; `[build]` this skill's choice, for the owner to confirm the first time it is used.

## 3. Facts the sheet needs, carried here

From the pack's notes, read 2026-10-20. Cite the notes section when one is used.

Disclosure wording (notes s.1). Works: "ad", "advertisement", "sponsored", "#ad", "Ad:", "Paid ad"; "Paid link" next to an affiliate link. Does not work on its own: "sp", "spon", "collab", "thanks", "ambassador", "partner", "#comped", "#hosted"; "Gifted" alone is ambiguous; "affiliate link" alone is not enough. Placement: with the endorsement, not on a profile page, not after a "more" fold, not inside a run of hashtags. Video: in the video itself, said and shown; in a live stream, repeated. A platform's tool is used as well as the wording, never instead.

Partnership labels by platform (notes s.5). TikTok: the commercial content disclosure toggle is required. YouTube: the paid promotion setting adds a label at the start of the video. X: a native paid partnership label, from press coverage of March 2026. Instagram: the paid-partnership tool, marked unsourced in the notes; verify. Reddit: a "Brand Affiliate" tag, from secondary coverage only. Others: not in the references; verify.

Age settings by platform (notes s.6). Instagram: creators with the paid-partnership label can set a minimum age per post, which cannot be added or changed after publishing; professional accounts can also set an account-wide minimum. YouTube: "restrict my videos to viewers over 18" in YouTube Studio; YouTube warns that age-restricted videos may earn little or no ad revenue and that videos meant as ads should not use it. TikTok: audience controls can mark a video or LIVE as 18 and over. X: mark media as sensitive. Facebook Pages: an age restriction in the Page settings. OnlyFans, Reddit, podcasts, newsletters, Bluesky, Threads: not in the references; verify.

Who answers for what (notes s.1). Advertisers are liable for misleading or unsubstantiated statements made through endorsements and for undisclosed connections (16 CFR 255.1(d)); endorsers may be liable for what they say (255.1(e)); agencies and other intermediaries may be liable for their part (255.1(f)); using an agency does not move the brand's duty to train and monitor. Inferred, for counsel to confirm: a private indemnity between the parties does not change who a regulator can hold responsible.

Product categories. Optional restricted-category research lives in shared policy notes sections 3 and 4. Verify applicability for the actual product; no regulatory classification is presumed for the programme.
