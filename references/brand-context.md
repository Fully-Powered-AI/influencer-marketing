# Owner-supplied brand context

This package has no built-in brand facts. Keep business inputs in your runtime workspace, outside this package. `program/program.md` is the shared source of decisions. Start it with `program-setup`; preserve its numbered headings, field labels and table headers because the scripts parse them.

## 1. Brand and product evidence

Fill section 1 with the exact **Brand name** (used to time spoken disclosures), a one-line description, the products in scope, product category, variants, materials or ingredients, prices and currency. Record dated source URLs and the owner's confirmations. Store supporting product evidence in `program/brand-facts.md` or `exports/`; record the pointer in the programme file. Neither a public page nor an example is approval to make a claim.

The owner supplies audience, markets, languages, brand voice, competitor names and search vocabulary. Discovery uses these inputs; it does not infer a person's sensitive traits from a profile.

## 2. Claims and contradictions

Compare current labels, product pages, technical specifications and owner evidence for each product. Section 7 has four generic review prompts, not known problems with any real brand. Record `none found; <sources checked and date> [owner]` where a prompt does not apply. An unresolved conflict keeps affected claims out of Approved.

Write an approved statement as `- Canvas Tote: "Cotton canvas construction" [owner]`. This is fictional example wording, not a fact about your products. Forbidden lines quote the exact phrase to catch, followed by an unquoted reason. The review still covers paraphrases and unlisted claims.

An optional section 7 field configures English claim-review cues for the caption, outreach and onboarding checks:

```markdown
- Claim review cues: "waterproof"; "recycled"; "certified" [owner]
```

A settled `none [owner]` disables heuristic cues, not approved/forbidden claim checks or human review. Missing cues use generic defaults. A cue is a request for review, not a compliance finding. Category-specific words belong here only when relevant to the products in scope. The onboarding kit carries operational instructions; product claims belong in the brief.

## 3. Category, markets and audiences

Section 2 determines markets and applicable jurisdictions; do not default every brand to US rules. Verify the sources applicable to the actual category, market, platform and placement before filling section 3. Organic posts, paid amplification and platform commerce are separate decisions. Dated policy notes are leads, not permission.

Section 9 carries content suitability, audience restrictions, evidence, redlines and voice. For ordinary home goods an owner might record `none; general home-goods audience, no age restriction identified in the verified rules [owner]` after the Audience age label. A restricted-category programme might instead specify a minimum age and evidence required. These are alternative owner decisions, not pack-wide rules. `confirmed` in publication tracking means evidence meets the configured rule. `not-required` is appropriate only for a programme that explicitly requires none; a missing or open rule remains unchecked, and an unavailable setting does not satisfy a required restriction.

## 4. Example data

Cedar Lane is an invented home-goods brand used in fixtures. Other example brands, creators, campaigns, contacts, products and commercial values are fictional. Example URLs use example.com or its subdomains. Some fixtures deliberately configure restrictive audience rules to test that branch; they do not establish rules for home goods or for any platform.
